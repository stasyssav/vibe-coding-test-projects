About RV/PA coupling

The right ventricle has to adapt its contractility to the load imposed by the pulmonary circulation. As long as it can, the two are said to be coupled. When pulmonary pressure rises faster than the ventricle can compensate, the RV dilates, its function falls, and the system becomes uncoupled. Invasively, coupling is measured as the ratio of end-systolic elastance to arterial elastance (Ees/Ea) from pressure–volume loops.

The TAPSE/sPAP ratio is a simple echocardiographic surrogate: TAPSE reflects longitudinal RV contraction, and sPAP reflects afterload. A ratio around 0.31 mm/mmHg or lower has been shown to identify RV–PA uncoupling against invasive Ees/Ea. The 2022 ESC/ERS pulmonary hypertension guidelines use the ratio for risk stratification with three strata: above 0.32 (low risk), 0.19 to 0.32 (intermediate risk), and below 0.19 (high risk).

For educational use only. It does not replace clinical judgement, full echocardiographic assessment, or right heart catheterisation. Estimated sPAP is unreliable with severe tricuspid regurgitation or a poor Doppler envelope.
